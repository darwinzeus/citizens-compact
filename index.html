The Citizen's Compact
for 2029

To: Fellow Citizens of India considering candidacy for Parliament From: A Fellow Citizen of India

"True development is measured by the amount of time a common citizen saves in a day, the peace of mind they feel in public spaces, and the dignity with which they interact with their own government."

Taking inspiration from the world's happiest, most efficient, and highly developed nations, I submit this Citizen's Compact. For the upcoming 2029 elections, my vote — and the votes of millions of like-minded citizens — is contingent on you explicitly adopting these actionable, low-cost, high-impact reforms into your official election manifesto.

TIER 1

Immediate Daily Relief

Immediate Legislation

01

Mandated "Honest Pricing" & Standardized Unit Weights

Consumer Protection

The Policy

Legally require all consumer goods (FMCG) and e-commerce platforms to display the "Price per Unit" (e.g., ₹/gram or ₹/ml) in bold, right next to the Maximum Retail Price (MRP).

The Reason

This immediately eliminates "shrinkflation" — the deceptive corporate practice of reducing product volume while keeping the price and packaging identical. It empowers the average shopper to spot bad deals instantly and protects household budgets.

02

The "Right to Walk" — Pedestrian-First Urban Infrastructure

Urban Planning

The Policy

Legally mandate that a fixed, unalterable percentage of all municipal road development budgets be spent exclusively on continuous, encroachment-free, paved sidewalks and safe, well-lit zebra crossings.

The Reason

Walkability is a basic human right. Forcing civil engineering standards to prioritize humans over cars will drastically reduce pedestrian fatalities, decrease local traffic congestion, and make our cities safer for children and senior citizens.

03

The "Right to Disconnect" for Work-Life Balance

Labor Rights

The Policy

Introduce labor guidelines protecting employees from being penalized, explicitly or implicitly, for ignoring work-related emails, texts, or calls outside of officially designated working hours.

The Reason

Chronic work stress and digital hyper-connectivity are fueling a massive mental health crisis among India's working class. Setting clear boundaries preserves family time and mental well-being without costing the public exchequer a single rupee.

04

Crowdsourced Traffic Enforcement with Citizen Rewards

Public Safety

The Policy

Launch a centralized, secure mobile application allowing citizens to submit video or photographic evidence of traffic violations. Upon automated and manual verification of the number plate, a challan is issued, and 20% of the recovered fine is instantly credited to the reporting citizen's bank account (capped at ₹5,000/month).

The Reason

Individuals comply with traffic laws only when there is a high probability of being caught. Empowering millions of citizens creates an inescapable net of accountability — instilling traffic discipline overnight and drastically reducing fatal accidents without increasing government enforcement budgets.

TIER 2

Public Health & Infrastructure

Administrative Reform

05

Mandatory Front-of-Pack Warning Labels on Processed Foods

Public Health

The Policy

Require all packaged and processed foods to display prominent, standardized front-of-pack warning labels when they exceed scientifically defined thresholds for added sugar, sodium, or saturated fat. Labels such as "High in Sugar," "High in Salt," and "High in Saturated Fat" — take inspiration from Chile's proven model.

The Reason

India is rapidly becoming the diabetes and cardiovascular disease capital of the world. Citizens deserve clear, unambiguous information that cuts through misleading marketing and empowers families to make informed food decisions within seconds.

06

Strict Caps & Transparency on School Fees

Education

The Policy

Enforce a legally binding cap on private school fees and ban all hidden, mandatory tertiary charges — such as forcing parents to buy specific uniforms, books, or materials exclusively from the school at inflated rates.

The Reason

Quality education has become a commercialized extortion trap, ranking as one of the top three financial anxieties for the Indian middle class. Predictable, transparent fee structures provide immediate economic breathing room for families.

07

Prohibition of Public Road Blockages for Minor VIP Movements

Civic Dignity

The Policy

Restrict the privilege of halting public traffic for official convoys exclusively to the President, Prime Minister, and state Chief Ministers. All other public servants must navigate regular traffic. A dedicated ambulance lane must remain unobstructed during all such events.

The Reason

Millions of productive citizen hours are wasted daily, and medical emergencies are delayed, to accommodate minor local dignitaries. Eliminating this feudal practice restores dignity and time back to the taxpaying public.

08

The "Fact-Only" Media Mandate — Separation of News & Opinion

Media Integrity

The Policy

Legally mandate that news channels must clearly segregate "Factual News Reporting" from "Opinion/Debate Commentary." Any opinion-based show must carry a permanent on-screen disclaimer labeling it as "Commentary, Not News."

The Reason

Mainstream news has degraded into polarizing, opinion-driven theater that fuels social anxiety, misinformation, and communal division. Forcing media to return to verification-first journalism restores public trust and ensures citizens make choices based on facts, not manufactured outrage.

TIER 3

Systemic Political Accountability

Constitutional & Judicial Upgrades

09

Mandatory Government Schooling for Children of Public Officials

Systemic Reform

The Policy

Pass legislation requiring that children of all central government officers and elected Members of Parliament attend state-run government schools.

The Reason

The fastest way to fix public infrastructure is to ensure that policymakers have skin in the game. The moment their children sit in public classrooms, the political will to fund, secure, and upgrade public education will skyrocket overnight.

10

Performance-Based Continuity & Right to Recall for MPs

Electoral Reform

The Policy

Implement a 6-month probationary period for elected MPs, an annual "Right to Recall" mechanism for constituencies, and a strict rule mandating a minimum of 75% parliamentary attendance to be eligible for the next election cycle (with the same attendance mandate for Rajya Sabha).

The Reason

This permanently eliminates political complacency. Representatives must treat their seats like performance-evaluated jobs where they remain constantly answerable to their constituents, rather than disappearing for five years.

11

Mandatory Quarterly Open Press Conferences by All MPs

Transparency

The Policy

Legally obligate every elected MP to hold an open-floor, unscripted quarterly press conference within their home constituency (minimum 4 hours), accessible to local media and resident welfare associations. Mandate that 100% of MPLADS allocations, project approvals, contractor names, timelines, and raw invoices be updated on a publicly accessible, real-time geographic dashboard.

The Reason

Citizens shouldn't have to file complex RTI requests to see how their local tax money is being utilized. Total transparency on local spending kills corruption at the root and lets voters judge their MP on concrete development data.

12

No Images of Current or Former MPs on Government Media

Institutional Integrity

The Policy

Prohibit the display of photographs, portraits, names, signatures, slogans, or personal branding of any current or former MP on government-funded advertisements, public notices, welfare scheme materials, billboards, websites, social media, and official documents.

The Reason

Government programs are funded by citizens and administered by public institutions, not by individual politicians. Public communication should inform citizens about services, rights, and policies — not serve as personal election campaigns funded by taxpayer money.

TIER 4

The Future of Citizen Participation

Technological Innovation

13

Secure Blockchain-Enabled "One Nation, One Election"

Democratic Tech

The Policy

Develop an open-source decentralized, secure electronic voting infrastructure utilizing multi-factor digital authentication backed by a public blockchain ledger, employing end-to-end verifiability and zero-knowledge cryptography. A voter can verify their ballot was cast and counted accurately without compromising the absolute secrecy of the ballot. Traditional EVMs will remain as a parallel, physical alternative. Life imprisonment for those creating deceptive imitation apps or websites.

The Reason

Synchronizing election cycles reduces massive public spending and administrative paralysis. An immutable digital ledger provides unparalleled transparency, eliminates voter suppression, and makes democratic participation accessible, verifiable, and fraud-proof. If we can use OTP and fingerprint for daily transactions, why not for voting?

The modern Indian citizen demands a life with less friction, more safety, absolute transparency, and genuine respect for their time and hard-earned tax money.

— To All the Citizens of the Country
